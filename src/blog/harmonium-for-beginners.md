## Your Harmonium Journey Starts Here

Learning the harmonium is one of the most rewarding musical journeys you can undertake. Whether you're drawn to Indian classical music, devotional singing, or simply want to explore a unique instrument, this guide will set you on the right path.

## Understanding the Basics

### What Makes the Harmonium Special?

The harmonium is unique among keyboard instruments because:

- It produces sound through **free reeds** (like an accordion), not strings or pipes
- It requires **hand-pumped bellows** to generate airflow
- It has a **sustained tone** that's ideal for vocal accompaniment
- It can produce a **continuous drone**, essential for Indian music

### The Notes of the Harmonium

The harmonium follows the standard 12-note chromatic scale. In Indian classical music, these notes are called **swaras**:

- **Shadja (Sa)** — The root note
- **Rishabh (Re)** — Second note
- **Gandhar (Ga)** — Third note
- **Madhyam (Ma)** — Fourth note
- **Pancham (Pa)** — Fifth note
- **Dhaivat (Dha)** — Sixth note
- **Nishad (Ni)** — Seventh note

Each note can be natural (shuddha) or altered (komal/teevra), giving you the full chromatic range.

## Your First Lesson: Playing a Scale

### On a Physical Harmonium

1. Sit comfortably with the harmonium in front of you
2. Open the bellows by pulling the handle
3. Pump the bellows steadily with your left hand
4. Press the first key (Sa) with your right hand
5. Play each note in sequence: Sa, Re, Ga, Ma, Pa, Dha, Ni, Sa

### On Web Harmonium

If you don't have a physical instrument, you can practice online:

1. Go to [webharmonium.site](/)
2. Press keys A, S, D, F, G, H, J, K in order
3. Each key corresponds to one note of the scale

## Building Your Skills

### Week 1-2: Scales and Familiarity

- Practice the major scale daily
- Get comfortable with the keyboard layout
- Focus on even timing and steady rhythm

### Week 3-4: Simple Songs

- Try playing familiar melodies
- Start with children's songs or simple bhajans
- Don't worry about speed — accuracy matters more

### Month 2: Adding Expression

- Learn to vary the pressure for dynamics
- Practice slides (meend) between notes
- Try playing with different tempos

### Month 3 and Beyond: Ragas

- Begin learning basic ragas
- Study the ascending (arohan) and descending (avarohan) patterns
- Practice with recordings of experienced musicians

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Rushing** — Don't try to play fast before you can play accurately
2. **Skipping scales** — Scale practice builds the foundation for everything else
3. **Ignoring rhythm** — Use a metronome or tabla track to develop timing
4. **Not listening** — Train your ear by listening to harmonium recordings regularly
